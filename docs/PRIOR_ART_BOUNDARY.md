# AIFC Prior-Art Boundary

## Status

**Composition-level novelty: candidate / under review.**

AIFC does not claim that its mathematical or engineering components are individually new.

## Known component families

The following ideas have established prior art and are treated as inputs to AIFC rather than discoveries of AIFC:

- predicting or guessing before a later randomized event;
- commit-now / future-randomness-beacon constructions;
- public and certified randomness beacons;
- conditional min-entropy and guessing probability;
- causal DAGs, d-separation, and the global Markov property;
- martingales, Ville's inequality, e-values, and e-processes;
- sequential testing and optional-stopping-valid inference;
- secure logging, rollback protection, monotonic state, and freshness roots;
- transparency logs and gossip against split views;
- Byzantine quorum intersection;
- cryptographic commitments and digital signatures;
- theoretical retrocausal / closed-timelike-curve communication models.

## Candidate contribution under review

The research contribution being evaluated is narrower:

> **A single fail-closed, proof-carrying operational protocol that composes causal isolation, post-freeze target generation, history-wise entropy, exact candidate multiplicity, anytime-valid inference, rollback-resistant freshness, Byzantine witness continuity, and explicit evidence grades specifically to test claims that exact information about a future random target existed before that target was generated.**

The protocol does not assume a retrocausal channel in advance and remains scientifically useful when every experiment returns a null result.

## What has not been established

The following statements are **not** currently made:

```text
AIFC_IS_FIRST_METHOD_EVER = NOT_ESTABLISHED
AIFC_GLOBAL_NOVELTY = NOT_ESTABLISHED
AIFC_PATENT_NOVELTY = NOT_ESTABLISHED
NEW_MATHEMATICS = NO_CLAIM
PHYSICAL_RETROCAUSALITY = NOT_OBSERVED
```

A targeted prior-art sweep performed during the originating JANUS research line did not identify an exact end-to-end match, but absence of a match in a targeted search is not proof of worldwide novelty.

## Closest conceptual neighborhoods

A serious novelty review should search at least:

1. precognition / presentiment experimental methodology;
2. delayed-choice and retrocausal experimental proposals;
3. public randomness beacon challenge protocols;
4. verifiable delay / future randomness constructions;
5. cryptographic timestamping and commitment schemes;
6. secure logging and rollback-resistant evidence systems;
7. sequential e-value / e-process methodology;
8. causal-inference protocols for hidden common causes;
9. transparency and Byzantine witness systems;
10. patents combining future randomness with precommitment or evidentiary timestamps.

## Publication language

Until a broader scholarly and patent review is complete, the preferred public language is:

> **AIFC is a candidate-novel end-to-end methodology. In a targeted prior-art search, we found prior art for its individual components but did not identify an exact match for the full evidence stack. Global scientific novelty is not yet established.**

Avoid:

> "The world's first proof of information from the future."

Avoid:

> "AIFC proves retrocausality."

Prefer:

> "AIFC defines what evidence would be required to test such a claim without silently accepting leakage, multiplicity, post-selection, rollback, or optional-stopping explanations."

## Why this boundary matters

AIFC is intended to be useful to skeptical researchers.

Its scientific value should not depend on winning a priority contest. If an earlier equivalent protocol is found, it should be cited and the novelty claim narrowed. If a missing attack is found, it should be added to the threat model. If a theorem premise fails, the verifier should fail closed.

The project treats successful falsification of its own novelty or assumptions as useful research output.
